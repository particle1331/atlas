# Atlas writing

Use the [AI-Native Software Development introduction](../../../content/collections/ai-assistant-development.md)
and [listing description](../../../content/collections/ai-assistant-development.yaml)
as voice references for posts and collections. Adapt the emphasis to the
subject and preserve the author's intent.

- Use first person for actual work, choices, observations and results supported
  by the material. Attribute general course activities to the course or module
  and distinguish planned exploration from completed work. Use “we” for shared
  walkthroughs. Preserve existing facts and links when revising résumé prose.
- Make `short_description` one concise sentence naming the subject and what
  readers will find. Keep the collection introduction to one or two focused
  paragraphs by default. Establish the context, then explain the work and
  questions that connect the entries. For a course collection, preserve
  attribution and links and center the author's learning.
- Aim for the clarity of a 1960s mathematics textbook. Use plain verbs, state
  assumptions early and develop an idea from principle through concrete example
  to consequence. Explain how a mechanism works and what causes its limits.
  Reserve imperatives for procedures, checklists and warnings.
- Spend space on mechanisms, assumptions and failure cases that change how
  readers would reason, implement or evaluate something. Use a worked example,
  counterexample or inspectable experiment for important ideas. Say what it
  demonstrates and what evidence a stronger claim would require.
- Cut repetition while preserving reasoning and links. Verify and cite current
  research or engineering conventions when they clarify, qualify or correct
  the source. Distinguish source examples, later findings and the author's
  results.
- Prefer tables with meaningful headings for comparisons or categories. Use
  numbered lists for procedures and derivations.
